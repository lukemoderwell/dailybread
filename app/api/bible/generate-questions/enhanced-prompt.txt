🎯 THE DAILYBREAD QUESTION EXCELLENCE FRAMEWORK

1. **HIGHER-ORDER THINKING** (Beyond Surface-Level)

   ❌ AVOID Shallow Questions:
   - "What did [character] do?" (mere factual recall)
   - "Who said this?" (trivia)
   - Simple yes/no questions
   - Questions with one obvious answer

   ✅ AIM FOR Transformative Questions:
   - **ANALYZE**: "Why do you think [character] chose that? What was going on in their heart?"
   - **EVALUATE**: "Was that the right choice? What makes something right or wrong here?"
   - **CREATE**: "If you were going to live this out tomorrow, what would it look like?"
   - **CONNECT**: "When have you felt exactly like [character] did?"
   - **APPLY**: "What's ONE thing you could do this week because of this passage?"
   - **WONDER**: "What does this teach us about who God is?"

2. **THE "DISCOVERY SPARK"** - Every Question Needs One

   Exceptional questions contain an element that creates wonder:
   - **Surprise**: "What's the most unexpected/shocking part of this?"
   - **Personal relevance**: "Where do YOU show up in this story?"
   - **Paradox**: "How can [seeming contradiction] both be true?"
   - **Mystery**: "What's puzzling about this? What makes you curious?"
   - **Emotion**: "How do you think [character] felt? Have you ever felt that way?"
   - **God's character**: "What does this reveal about what God is like?"

3. **QUESTION DIVERSITY MATRIX** - Cover Multiple Dimensions

   Across the family, strategically include:

   **A) THEOLOGICAL DEPTH**
   - Who is God in this passage? (His character, heart, actions)
   - What does this teach about God's ways?
   - How does this point to Jesus/the Gospel?

   **B) PERSONAL APPLICATION**
   - "How does this change how I should live?"
   - "What would be different in my life if I really believed this?"
   - Concrete, age-appropriate action steps

   **C) EMOTIONAL CONNECTION**
   - Empathy with characters
   - Identifying personal feelings
   - Processing difficult emotions through Scripture

   **D) IMAGINATION & CREATIVITY**
   - "What if..." scenarios
   - "Imagine you were there..."
   - Creative problem-solving

   **E) REAL-WORLD BRIDGE**
   - School situations
   - Friend dynamics
   - Family challenges
   - Current struggles

   Each family member's question should explore a DIFFERENT dimension - together they paint a complete picture.

4. **CONVERSATION CATALYSTS** - Not Knowledge Tests

   Great questions are OPEN-ENDED and naturally lead to discussion:

   ✅ Questions should:
   - Have multiple valid, thoughtful answers
   - Invite personal stories and experiences
   - Allow "I wonder..." responses
   - Build on each other naturally in conversation
   - Create safe space for honest wrestling

   ❌ Avoid:
   - Bible trivia / knowledge tests
   - Questions that telegraph the "right" answer
   - Overly abstract theological jargon
   - Questions that shut down discussion quickly

5. **FAITH-BUILDING DNA** - Ultimate Purpose

   Every single question should actively develop:

   - **TRUST**: Does this help them trust God more? See His goodness?
   - **LOVE**: Does this deepen affection for God and His word?
   - **KNOW GOD**: Does this reveal who God truly is?
   - **DISCIPLESHIP**: Does this show them how to follow Jesus?
   - **COURAGE**: Does this equip them to live out faith?
   - **JOY**: Does this show why following God is the best life?

6. **THE "STICKINESS" FACTOR** - Memorable Impact

   Questions should be so good that:
   - Kids think about them later that day
   - They bring it up in other conversations
   - They see real-life connections throughout the week
   - The insight shapes how they think/act
   - They remember the discussion years later

AGE-APPROPRIATE GUIDELINES:

**Ages 2-3**:
- Very simple, concrete words (5-7 word questions)
- Focus on emotions, actions, what they can see
- "Was [character] happy or sad? Why?"
- "What did you see in the story?"
- Connect to their immediate world (family, pets, favorite things)
- Use repetition and rhythm when helpful

**Ages 4-5**:
- Simple but start introducing "why"
- Choices characters made
- Basic feelings and friendships
- "Why did [character] do that?"
- "How can we be like [character]?"
- "When do we need to [virtue from passage]?"

**Ages 6-8**:
- Begin deeper "why" and "how"
- Imagination: "What would YOU do?"
- Connect to their daily experiences
- Start introducing application
- "If this happened at school, what would you do?"
- "Why do you think God wants us to know this?"

**Ages 9-12**:
- Solid "why" and "how" about meaning
- Motivations, consequences, principles
- Real applications to their world
- "What would change if everyone did this?"
- "How does this help with [specific challenge]?"
- Beginning to handle nuance and complexity

**Ages 13+**:
- Challenging, sophisticated questions
- Theology, apologetics, worldview
- Cultural context and interpretation
- Ethical dilemmas and hard questions
- "How does this challenge our culture's view of..."
- "What's the difference between [related concepts]?"
- "How would you defend this to a skeptic?"
